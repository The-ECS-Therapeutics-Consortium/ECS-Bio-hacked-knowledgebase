Repository Overview
This repository serves as the public research and applied systems archive of the ECS Therapeutic Consortium, consolidating work focused on endocannabinoid-system–centred biological regulation across immunity, metabolism, neurobiology, and cellular stress dynamics.

The materials here span theoretical frameworks, mechanistic models, datasets, and applied protocol architectures, developed over multiple years and released as timestamped scholarly artefacts.

The repository is intended to function as:
a reference layer for ECS-oriented systems research
a technical archive supporting downstream applications and tooling
a foundation for licensing and collaboration built on verifiable prior art

All content is organised to support traceability, reproducibility, and external review.

## Architecture & Intent
Scope and Positioning
The work collected here focuses on systems-level organisation, not individual interventions.

It documents how biological regulation may be structured and reasoned about, without asserting outcomes or recommendations.

This repository operates as an applied research layer and is architecturally separate from any foundational kernels or constraint systems (e.g. QTOS). No kernel logic or invariant enforcement is defined here.

## System Coverage (High-Level)

The Consortium’s work spans multiple biological abstraction layers and is organised to support cross-domain reasoning rather than isolated datasets.

At a high level, the research corpus integrates:

- natural-world compound and modulation datasets  
- epigenetic and chromatin-level regulation (including histone dynamics)  
- enzymatic and signalling cascades  
- cryptic and non-obvious functional regulatory sequences  
- subcellular structures and organelle-level processes  
- cell-type–specific contexts across diverse tissues  
- disease and phenotype mappings across oncology and non-oncological domains  

These layers are intentionally structured for **axis-based unification**, enabling consistent reasoning across targets, mechanisms, cellular context, and disease labels without coupling the repository to any single protocol, outcome, or application.

Detailed schemas and mappings are documented within the repository artefacts themselves.
